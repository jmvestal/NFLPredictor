Where's your price from?
Implied vs. true probability
Implied probabilityThe sportsbook's chance, hidden in the odds: how often the bet has to win just to break even at that price.
True probabilityHow often it actually wins. Nobody knows it exactly; this app uses our tested model (or a system's tested win rate).
EdgeTrue − implied. Positive = a good bet over time. Negative = you lose over time, even on bets that usually win.
1
Turn odds into implied probability
Minus odds (favorites): odds ÷ (odds + 100), ignoring the minus sign.
−400 → 400 ÷ 500 = 80% · −150 → 150 ÷ 250 = 60%
Plus odds (underdogs): 100 ÷ (odds + 100).
+150 → 100 ÷ 250 = 40% · +300 → 100 ÷ 400 = 25%
Kalshi and Polymarket show it directly: 80¢ = 80%.
Decimal odds (Novig shows these): 1 ÷ the odds.
1.25 → 1 ÷ 1.25 = 80% · 1.77 → 56.5% · 2.50 → 40%
2
Compare it to the true chance
At −400 you risk $400 to win $100, so the bet must win more than 80% of the time to make money.
True 88% − implied 80% = +8% edge ✅
True 75% − implied 80% = −5% ❌ (wins most of the time, still loses money)
3
Why "past hit rate" isn't the true chance
LINEMAKER uses a player's past hit rate as the true chance. In our tests on 2021–26 games, that ran 5–10 points too high, while our model's % matched what actually happened. That's why the app uses the model.
4
How to use it here
Tap any ladder rung, system bet or prop, type your sportsbook's price, and the app does the math: ✅ Bet (edge 1.5%+), ⚠️ Thin (0–1.5%), ❌ Skip (negative). The "fair price" is the true chance turned back into odds. Any price better than it has an edge.

How it works: Team A's projected points = average of (A's Offensive PPP × A's offensive plays/game) and (B's Defensive PPP × A's offensive plays/game). Each team's PPP and plays/game include 4 games' worth of last season's averages, which fade as this season's games pile up. Tick "Backup QB" on a team starting someone other than its usual QB to take 3 points off its projection. Win probability comes from the projected margin (normal distribution, Margin SD). Fair ML is the no-vig moneyline for that probability. Edge = model win % − sportsbook no-vig win %; EV is per $100. A projection, not a guarantee — bet responsibly.

ScoresPoints this offense would score in a normal game against an average defense
AllowsPoints this defense would give up against an average offense (lower is better)

Off / Def PPPPoints per play scored / allowed: the two numbers everything is built from
Tap a column name to sort. Tap a team (or ⓘ How it works) to see how its numbers are calculated.
Off PPP = points scored ÷ own offensive plays. Def PPP = points allowed ÷ opponent offensive plays (lower is better). PPP and Plays/G blend this season with last season (see "Last-season games"); the season totals are this season only, so dividing Pts by Plays won't match Off PPP. Points include defensive/special-teams scores.
How to use: type the sportsbook's line (e.g. 64.5) in the Line box to see the model's chance of the Over and Under. Optionally add the book's Over/Under odds (e.g. -115 / -105) to compare against the book's own estimate with its cut removed.
How it works: Proj = the player's recent games (newer games count more, last season counts a little), pulled slightly toward typical values, adjusted for how much of that stat the opponent allows. The Over/Under chances come from the real spread of outcomes in 2021–23 games, and were checked on 2024–26 games: when it said 45%, it happened 45–47% of the time.
Good to know: the Median is usually below the Proj (average) for yards — most games land under a player's average, and a few big games pull it up. That's why an Over at a player's average line hits only ~42–44% for receivers and running backs. For star players (top 10%), the Over chance runs ~3–5 points low on receiving yards, rushing yards and receptions — they beat their projection a bit more often than the model expects. Props usually carry a bigger cut (−115 both ways ≈ 53.5% to break even). Whether these edges make money is untested — no free historical prop lines exist.
